# Phase D — MoneyFlow Brand Strategy

**Status:** accepted/completed brand-strategy authority
**Baseline:** `main@4ba1864ccadfdd4d8363815ba2a9eafc1a373c88`
**Scope:** brand meaning, positioning, promise, trust, personality and verbal
behaviour. **No** visual decision of any kind.
**Supersedes:** nothing. It *promotes* named elements of earlier brand material and
leaves the rest historical.

Phase D answers who MoneyFlow is for, what role it plays, what it promises, why that
promise is believable, how it differs, how it should speak, and what it must never
become. It does not answer what MoneyFlow should look like.

Throughout, **SOURCE FACT** is something observed in the repository or in a cited
external source. **DECISION** is a Phase D choice. They are never merged.

---

## 1. Executive brand strategy

MoneyFlow's brand rests on a single mechanism:

> **Every figure MoneyFlow shows can be traced back to a record the user entered
> themselves, and every ordinary mistake in those records can be corrected or
> recovered.**

That is the whole strategy. It is not a feeling, a tone or an aesthetic — it is a
property the product either has or does not have, and today it largely has it.

The Vietnamese category earns trust the opposite way. The two largest apps in this
space promise *removal of effort* and back it with *institutional scale*:
"liên kết ngân hàng, mọi giao dịch tự cập nhật", "chụp hóa đơn — AI tự phân loại",
"Biết tiền đi đâu — không cần tự nhớ", and, as reassurance, "30 năm kinh nghiệm công
nghệ, 3.000 nhân sự" [S1]. Money Lover leads with connection breadth across many
countries' banks [S2, S3].

MoneyFlow can match none of that and should not try. It has no bank connections, no
classifier, no institution behind it, and no automated capture. **The strategic
consequence is that MoneyFlow's trust cannot be borrowed — it has to be demonstrated
row by row.** What the category treats as MoneyFlow's weakness (the user types it) is
the precise reason MoneyFlow can offer something the category structurally cannot:
there is no gap between what the software believes and what the user knows, because
the user is the source.

This is deliberately a *narrower* promise than the category's. MoneyFlow does not
claim to know the user's complete financial life; it claims that the part the user
chose to record is accurate, explainable and repairable. Anxiety is reduced by
traceability and reversibility, not by an assurance that nothing was missed.

The brand's job in every later phase is to keep that claim exactly the size of the
product.

## 2. Primary audience and jobs

Defined behaviourally. This section deliberately contains **no demographic precision**,
because the repository holds no user research that would support any.

### The primary user, in behavioural terms

A person in Vietnam managing their own money across cash, one or more bank accounts
and e-wallets, who wants to be able to answer *"where did my money actually go?"*
from something they trust, and who is willing to spend a few seconds per transaction
to get an answer they do not have to second-guess.

| Dimension | Phase D position | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Financial behaviour | Money moves through several owned places; some of it is cash, which no automation can see | Product supports user-declared accounts and manual entry; cash is the case bank sync cannot cover |
| Current pain | Not "I lack a chart." It is "the number in front of me and the reason for it are disconnected" | Phase C's aggregate contract exists precisely to close that gap |
| Desired control | To record, check, correct and keep their own record — not to be optimised | Product law: recoverable destructive actions, backup ownership |
| Sophistication | Comfortable with the idea of a ledger; **not** required to know accounting terms, envelope budgeting or any method before the first entry | Product law: no method prerequisite; planning is optional |
| Relationship with manual entry | Accepts it as the price of provenance, and expects entry to be genuinely fast in exchange | Manual-first is a strategic choice, not a temporary gap |
| Runtime relationship | May look before committing; demo mode must not pretend to be their data | Explicit demo/authenticated boundary |

### What this user does *not* need MoneyFlow to be

- a way to avoid entering data;
- an adviser that tells them what they may spend;
- a wealth or investment picture;
- a shared household system;
- a budgeting method they must adopt before recording anything;
- a motivator that rewards or scolds them.

### Honesty about audience evidence

**SOURCE FACT:** the repository's product law, route map and financial invariants
support every row of the table above — they describe what the product is built for.

**NOT EVIDENCED:** the existing brand document asserts this user "đã từng mở một app
tài chính… rồi bỏ ngang trước khi ghi được giao dịch thật đầu tiên" — a specific
abandonment story. No user research in this repository supports it. Phase D does
**not** adopt it. It is recorded here as an untested hypothesis that a later phase may
test, not as an audience fact.

**Also not evidenced:** age, income band, occupation, city tier, household structure.
Phase D asserts none of them, and a later creative phase must not invent them.

### Jobs, inherited from Phase C

Phase D does not re-derive the job hierarchy. It adopts Phase C's canonical set —
primary daily loop (record, know position, inspect/correct, understand a period),
supporting jobs (plan, maintain, export a view), exception jobs (advanced intake,
recover, protect data, administer) — and takes the **primary loop as the brand's
centre of gravity.** A brand expression that makes planning, import or reporting feel
like the product's identity contradicts the architecture beneath it.

## 3. Brand role

**DECISION: MoneyFlow's role is the user's trusted ledger — the place where what
actually happened to their money is written down, checkable and repairable.**

Role types considered and rejected as the *primary* role:

| Role type | Why not primary |
|---|---|
| Record keeper | Accurate but too passive; it omits that MoneyFlow must also *explain* a period and *repair* a mistake, which is where trust is actually won or lost |
| Financial control surface | Overclaims. A control surface implies MoneyFlow sees enough to be in control of; it sees only what was entered |
| Planning companion | Contradicts product law. Planning is optional and must never precede a correct ledger |
| **Trusted ledger** | **Selected.** Carries record, provenance, explanation and repair in one idea, and is exactly co-extensive with what the product can back |

The role is compatible with the five constraints it must satisfy:

- **manual-first** — a ledger is, by definition, written by its keeper;
- **trustworthy history** — a ledger's value is that past entries stay inspectable;
- **transparent scope** — a ledger covers the accounts it was told about, and says so;
- **recoverability** — a ledger tolerates correction; that is what distinguishes it
  from a receipt;
- **optional planning** — a plan can be laid *on* a ledger without the ledger needing it.

**Role boundary:** MoneyFlow is the user's ledger, not their accountant. It records
and explains; it does not advise, score or decide.

## 4. Positioning

> **For** a person in Vietnam who wants to know where their own money went and to
> trust the answer,
> **MoneyFlow is** a personal ledger they keep themselves,
> **that** makes every total traceable to the records behind it and every ordinary
> mistake correctable,
> **because** the user entered each record, the app never invents or rounds money,
> transfers never masquerade as spending, deletions are recoverable, and the whole
> account can be exported and restored by its owner,
> **unlike** money apps that earn trust by automating entry — where a total is
> something you can accept or ignore, but not check.

### Notes on the anchor

The "unlike" deliberately names a **failure mode, not a competitor.** The failure mode
is *unverifiable totals*, and it is not unique to any one product — a manual app can
fail this way too, by showing an aggregate with no path to its evidence. Phase C's
aggregate contract is what keeps MoneyFlow on the right side of its own positioning.

The positioning does **not** claim MoneyFlow is more complete, more automatic, more
secure, or better funded than anything else. It claims a different relationship
between the user and the number.

### Category frame

**DECISION:** MoneyFlow enters the category as **"sổ thu chi cá nhân"** (a personal
income-and-expense ledger), not as "ứng dụng quản lý tài chính" (a financial
management application). The narrower frame is the honest one and sets accurate
expectations at first contact. Note this is a *frame*, not a route label or tagline —
Phase C deliberately left labels open and Phase D does not close them.

## 5. Brand promise hierarchy

### Primary promise

> **Con số nào cũng lần được về dòng bạn đã ghi — và nhầm thì sửa được.**
> *(Every figure traces back to a record you wrote — and a mistake can be fixed.)*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Product proof** | Aggregates are computed from ledger rows by deterministic domain modules; reports carry period/scope; delete is soft with an undo window and restore; edits recompute by difference |
| **User benefit** | The user can act on a number without having to trust it blindly, and can afford to make an ordinary mistake |
| **Prohibited overclaim** | That MoneyFlow knows their *whole* financial position, that nothing is missing, or that every possible mistake is reversible (account deletion and an expired undo window are not) |

### Supporting promises

**P2 — Money keeps its meaning.**
- *Proof:* integer đồng end to end; a decimal separator is rejected rather than silently reinterpreted; transfers are paired and neutral, excluded from income, expense and category spend.
- *Benefit:* the totals mean what the user thinks they mean; moving money between one's own accounts never looks like spending.
- *Prohibited:* claiming multi-currency sophistication, or that MoneyFlow validates real-world correctness of an amount the user typed.

**P3 — The data belongs to the person who entered it.**
- *Proof:* complete versioned archive with validation and an authenticated restore path; scoped export is separate and labelled as not a backup; authenticated state is tenant-scoped under RLS.
- *Benefit:* the ledger is not hostage to the product.
- *Prohibited:* "your data is safe with us" as a security guarantee; and — until the outstanding hosted-restore limitation is closed — any phrasing that implies restore has been proven against a live hosted account. See §13.

**P4 — Nothing pretends to be what it is not.**
- *Proof:* explicit demo versus authenticated runtime; demo refuses archive actions; a report export says it is not a complete backup; planning objects keep expectation and assumption distinct from posted facts.
- *Benefit:* the user is never misled about what they are looking at.
- *Prohibited:* presenting sample data as their money, or a projection as a fact.

**P5 — Planning is available, never required.**
- *Proof:* budgets, commitments, income templates and goals are separate optional objects; the ledger is fully usable with none of them.
- *Benefit:* no method to learn before the first entry.
- *Prohibited:* any promise of a "safe to spend" figure, savings outcome or optimisation.

### Promise ordering rule

If a later phase must drop something for space, it drops from **P5 upward**. The
primary promise is the last thing to go, because it is the only one the other four
serve.

## 6. Differentiation map

A feature is listed as a differentiator only where it changes what the user can
*believe*, not merely what they can *do*.

### Table stakes — necessary, never a selling point

Recording income and expense; categories; multiple accounts; a balance; a monthly
view; editing a record; light/dark; working on a phone. Every serious app in the
category has these. Claiming them as advantages would read as thin.

### Differentiators

| # | Differentiator | Why it actually differentiates | Honest cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | **Provenance by construction** — every row exists because the user entered it | Removes the "is this classification right?" question that automated capture creates by design | The user types. This is real effort and must be paid back with genuinely fast entry |
| D2 | **Traceable aggregates** — a total names its scope and leads to the records behind it | The category's totals are typically terminal; this one is a starting point | Requires discipline in every later surface; an untraceable total breaks the brand |
| D3 | **Correction as a first-class path** — soft delete, undo, edit-by-difference, restore | Most apps treat a mistake as the user's problem; MoneyFlow treats it as an expected event | The undo window is time-boxed; not every action is reversible |
| D4 | **Transfer neutrality** — moving your own money is never spending | A specific, checkable correctness property that users can and do get burned by elsewhere | Only meaningful if reports and overview both honour it |
| D5 | **Complete backup ownership, distinct from export** | The category rarely distinguishes these; MoneyFlow does, in code | Restore has a real, named limitation today (§13) |
| D6 | **Honest demo boundary** | A person can look before committing without their sample data ever being confused with real data | — |

### Anti-differentiators — true, but must not be sold as advantages

- **Manual entry as such.** "You type everything" is a cost, not a benefit. It is only
  a differentiator *through* D1 (provenance). Selling the typing itself is selling the
  price instead of the value.
- **Vietnamese-first.** Correct and necessary, but the category is already Vietnamese.
- **No bank connection.** An absence. It enables D1, but "we don't connect to your
  bank" as a headline reads as a missing feature.
- **Optional planning.** Restraint, not capability.
- **Advanced intake (Inbox/import/rules).** Present and useful, but Phase C fixes it as
  a secondary exception loop. Promoting it would contradict the architecture and
  invite comparison on exactly the automation axis MoneyFlow loses.

### The one-line difference

Others reduce the effort of recording. **MoneyFlow reduces the doubt.**

## 7. Trust strategy

Trust is not a tone. Each builder below is a behaviour with a proof; each breaker is a
behaviour that destroys more trust than any wording can rebuild.

### Trust builders

| # | Builder | How it is demonstrated |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | **Financial truth** | Integer đồng; no invented or rounded money; transfer neutrality; deterministic domain modules |
| T2 | **Traceability** | Every aggregate names its scope and reaches its evidence |
| T3 | **Recoverable mistakes** | Soft delete with undo, restore, edit-by-difference |
| T4 | **Transparent scope** | The product says what a number covers *and what it does not* — which accounts, which period, transfers excluded |
| T5 | **Stated uncertainty** | Missing data stays missing; a projection is labelled as one |
| T6 | **Data ownership** | Complete archive, validated, restorable by its owner; scoped export honestly labelled |
| T7 | **Boundary honesty** | Demo cannot touch real data; authenticated state is tenant-scoped; destructive account actions require recent authentication |
| T8 | **Error language that helps** | An error says what happened, whether anything changed, and what to do |

### Trust breakers

| # | Breaker | Why it is fatal |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | A financial figure that is wrong even briefly | Already observed on a real phone; a number that flashes wrong and corrects itself teaches the user not to trust any number |
| B2 | A total with no path to its records | Turns MoneyFlow into the thing its positioning rejects |
| B3 | A silent change to a user's input | The magnitude bug found on a real device: `12,5` saved as `125`. Silence is the defect, not the rejection |
| B4 | A transfer counted as spending | Breaks a property the brand explicitly claims |
| B5 | Advice phrased as fact | "Bạn có thể tiêu X hôm nay" is unbackable |
| B6 | Demo data presented as real | Contaminates the only thing the brand sells |
| B7 | Calling a scoped export a backup | A promise the export cannot keep, discovered at the worst moment |
| B8 | An irreversible action that looked ordinary | Recovery must be as visible as destruction |
| B9 | Blaming the user for a product failure | "Bạn đã nhập sai" where MoneyFlow accepted the input |

### The asymmetry

Every builder is cumulative and slow. Every breaker is instant and, for a financial
product, close to unrecoverable. **Phase E and later phases must treat B1–B9 as design
constraints, not copy guidance** — most of them are caused by architecture, not
wording.

## 8. Emotional transformation

Strategic only. These are **not** visual moods and must not be handed to Phase E as
atmosphere.

| Stage | State | The mechanism that moves it |
|---|---|---|
| **Before** | *Disconnected.* The user knows their balance dropped and cannot reconstruct why. Their memory is the only record, and they know it is unreliable | — |
| **During** | *Deliberate.* Recording is a small, fast, conscious act. The user knows exactly what was written, because they wrote it. Nothing is inferred on their behalf | Manual capture with immediate, correct feedback |
| **After** | *Oriented and unafraid.* The user can answer where money went for a period they chose, follow any number to its records, and fix a mistake without dread | Traceable aggregates + recoverable correction |

Three dimensions, stated as mechanisms:

- **guesswork → evidence** — the answer comes from records, not recall;
- **opacity → traceability** — a number is a starting point, not a verdict;
- **fear of mistakes → recoverable control** — an error is an expected, repairable event.

**The transformation MoneyFlow does not offer:** relief from having to record, and
confidence that nothing was missed. Both are outside what it can back.

## 9. Brand personality

Five traits. Each is behaviourally testable — you can look at a screen or a string and
say whether it complies. Vague adjectives (clean, modern, simple, premium, friendly)
are deliberately absent; they cannot fail a test.

### 1. Exact

- **Behaviour:** states the number, its scope and its basis; never approximates for comfort.
- **Language:** "Chi trong tháng 8, chưa gồm chuyển tiền" rather than "Bạn đã chi khá nhiều".
- **Product:** an aggregate declares period and account scope, and reaches its records.
- **NOT:** pedantic or dense. Exactness is about being *checkable*, not about showing more digits.
- **Test:** can the user tell what this number covers without opening anything else?

### 2. Unhurried

- **Behaviour:** never manufactures urgency; no streaks, countdowns or nudges to open the app.
- **Language:** no exclamation marks on financial outcomes; no "Nhanh lên", no "Đừng bỏ lỡ".
- **Product:** no engagement mechanic; the only time pressure that exists is the undo window, which protects the user.
- **NOT:** slow. Recording must be fast. Unhurried is about *pressure*, not speed.
- **Test:** does anything here push the user to act sooner than their own money requires?

### 3. Accountable

- **Behaviour:** when something fails, MoneyFlow says what happened, whether data changed, and what to do — and takes the blame when it is MoneyFlow's.
- **Language:** "Không lưu được giao dịch. Chưa có gì thay đổi." rather than "Đã xảy ra lỗi".
- **Product:** failures leave no partial state; refusals state that nothing changed.
- **NOT:** apologetic. One statement of fact, no grovelling.
- **Test:** after this message, does the user know whether their money moved?

### 4. Deferential

- **Behaviour:** the user decides; MoneyFlow records and explains. It offers evidence, never verdicts.
- **Language:** describes what happened, not what the user should do or feel; never "quá nhiều", "đáng lo".
- **Product:** no scores, grades, ratings or automatic judgements of spending.
- **NOT:** vague or unhelpful. Deference is about *authority*, not about withholding information.
- **Test:** is there any evaluation of the user's choices here that they did not ask for?

### 5. Legible

- **Behaviour:** Vietnamese that a person who has never studied accounting can read, without inventing cute names for real financial concepts.
- **Language:** "chuyển tiền giữa tài khoản của bạn", not "internal transfer" and not a coined metaphor.
- **Product:** concepts are named consistently everywhere (§11).
- **NOT:** dumbed down. A transfer is still a transfer.
- **Test:** would a first-time user and a returning user read this term the same way?

### Personality boundary

MoneyFlow must not read as a bank, a crypto product, a get-rich app, a game, a
spreadsheet, or an all-knowing assistant. **DECISION:** this boundary is *promoted*
from the existing brand guidelines, which state it in the same terms, because it
follows directly from product law rather than from a visual direction.

## 10. Verbal strategy

Vietnamese is the product language. These are principles for writing it, not a copy
library — Phase C left labels open and Phase D does not close them.

### Foundational rules

1. **Vietnamese first, and natively.** Write Vietnamese, do not translate English patterns. An English draft that is then translated produces stiff financial phrasing.
2. **The user is the actor.** "Bạn đã ghi", not "Hệ thống đã ghi nhận".
3. **Financial precision beats brevity.** Never trade the scope of a number for a shorter sentence.
4. **Say what changed.** Especially when nothing did.

### SAY THIS WAY / AVOID THIS WAY

| Situation | Say this way | Avoid this way | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sentence style | Short declaratives; one idea each | Marketing cadence, rhetorical questions | The domain is money |
| Terminology | One term per concept, everywhere | Synonym variety for "freshness" | Consistency *is* legibility here |
| A figure | Name period and scope: "Chi tháng 8 (chưa gồm chuyển tiền)" | A bare number, or "tổng chi tiêu của bạn" | Scope is the claim |
| User agency | "Bạn có thể sửa hoặc xoá" | "Chúng tôi sẽ tối ưu giúp bạn" | Deference |
| Errors | "Không lưu được. Chưa có gì thay đổi. Thử lại." | "Đã xảy ra lỗi", "Oops!" | The user needs the data state |
| The user's mistake | "Số tiền cần là số nguyên đồng" | "Bạn đã nhập sai" | Correct the input, not the person |
| Confirmations | Name the exact object and effect | "Bạn có chắc không?" | A confirmation with no content is noise |
| Destructive actions | State what is lost, what is recoverable, and for how long | "Hành động này không thể hoàn tác" when it can | Overstating danger is also dishonest |
| Planning | Mark expectation and assumption: "dự kiến", "giả định" | Presenting a projection in the same voice as a posted fact | P4 |
| Empty states | Say what this surface will hold and the one action that fills it | "Chưa có dữ liệu" alone; cheerful mascots | An empty state is the first teaching moment |
| Reports/aggregates | State period, scope and exclusions | "Tổng quan tài chính của bạn" | Overclaims coverage |
| Advanced features | Plain description of what it does and that review is required | Framing automation as intelligence | Contradicts D1 |
| Demo | Always say it is sample data | Letting demo copy read like the real product | B6 |

### Prohibited vocabulary

Unless product truth changes: "AI", "thông minh", "tự động phân loại" as a promise,
"an toàn tuyệt đối", "tối ưu tài chính", "có thể tiêu", "giàu", "miễn phí mãi mãi",
and any quantified savings outcome. Note the category *does* make quantified-savings
claims [S1]; MoneyFlow has no basis for one.

## 11. Controlled vocabulary

Semantic authority: what each concept **means** and what it must never be conflated
with. This is not route-label approval — Phase C left labels open, and Phase E/F may
choose different surface words provided the distinctions below survive.

| Concept | Means | Must never be conflated with |
|---|---|---|
| **Giao dịch** (transaction) | One recorded financial fact: expense, income or transfer | A plan, an expectation, or an unreviewed candidate |
| **Lịch sử / sổ** (history/ledger) | The inspectable record of what happened | A report; a report explains a slice of history |
| **Tài khoản** (account) | A place the user declared that money sits | A login/identity — see below |
| **Số dư** (balance) | What an account holds now, from its records | A spendable amount or a recommendation |
| **Chuyển tiền** (transfer) | Paired neutral movement between the user's own accounts | Income or expense, in any total |
| **Báo cáo** (report) | A scoped explanation of a selected period/scope | A complete financial picture; a backup |
| **Xuất dữ liệu** (scoped export) | A download of the *requested* view | A complete backup |
| **Bản sao lưu** (complete backup) | The full versioned archive of the account's data | A report export |
| **Khôi phục** (restore) | Rebuilding an account from a complete backup | Undo of one transaction |
| **Hoàn tác** (undo) | Reversing the record just deleted, within its window | Restore |
| **Kế hoạch** (planning) | Budgets, commitments, income templates, goals — expectations | Posted facts |
| **Dự kiến / giả định** | A projection or an assumption | An amount that has happened |
| **Inbox / nhập nâng cao** | Candidates awaiting review before becoming history | The ledger itself |
| **Quy tắc** (rules) | Deterministic user-defined handling of candidates | Intelligence or learning |
| **Chế độ dùng thử** (demo) | Sample data in the browser | The user's account |

### Two collisions to resolve deliberately

- **"Tài khoản"** currently means both *a money account* and *a user identity/login*.
  This is a real ambiguity in a product that has both. Phase D flags it; the resolution
  belongs to whichever later phase owns labels, and it must be resolved rather than
  inherited.
- **"Xoá"** applies to a transaction (recoverable), an account (archival), and the user
  account (destructive and permanent). These must not share undifferentiated wording.

## 12. Brand anti-positioning

MoneyFlow is **not**, and must not present itself as:

| # | Not this | Because |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | A bank-sync dashboard | No bank connection exists; the absence is what makes D1 possible |
| A2 | An AI financial adviser | No advice capability, and P5/B5 forbid the claim |
| A3 | An investment or wealth terminal | No holdings, prices or valuation |
| A4 | A household or shared finance network | Tenant-scoped, single-owner data |
| A5 | A full-envelope budgeting ideology | Planning is optional by product law |
| A6 | A gamified spending coach | Contradicts *Unhurried* and *Deferential*; no streaks, badges, scores |
| A7 | A generic fintech super-app | The narrow ledger frame is the positioning |
| A8 | An accounting or bookkeeping tool | Explicitly no accounting terminology prerequisite |
| A9 | A guarantee of data safety | Ownership tools are provided; absolute safety is not claimable |

A1–A7 are the mission's list. **A8 and A9 are added** on product-truth grounds: the
audience definition rules out an accounting prerequisite, and the trust strategy
forbids an absolute-safety claim.

Nothing is removed from the list.

## 13. Proof architecture

Every load-bearing claim, with what actually backs it and what would break it. **If a
row's proof disappears, the claim must be withdrawn before the next release, not
quietly kept.**

| Claim | Executable/product proof | User-visible proof | Limitation | Risk if product changes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Figures trace to records | Deterministic domain modules compute from ledger rows; report scope contracts | Aggregate names scope and drills into filtered records | Only covers what the user recorded | A new surface showing an unlinked total silently voids the primary promise |
| Money keeps its meaning | Integer đồng throughout; fraction input rejected, not reinterpreted; safe-integer bounds | Rejected input stays visible with a stated reason | Correctness of the amount itself is the user's | Any future rounding or float path breaks P2 |
| Transfers are neutral | Paired movement excluded from income/expense and category spend, in domain and reports | Transfer rows appear under their own type and in neither income nor expense totals | Only for accounts the user declared | A convenience aggregate that folds transfers in breaks D4 |
| Mistakes are recoverable | Soft delete + restore; time-boxed undo; edit recomputes by difference | Undo action on the delete notice; edited row shows new values without duplicating | The undo window expires; account deletion is permanent | Removing the window without a replacement path breaks D3 |
| The account is exportable and restorable | Versioned complete archive with strict validation; authenticated restore; separate scoped export | Backup surface distinct from report export, labelled | **Hosted restore has never been executed against a live account.** Id preservation refuses a restore while the source account is still live; the round trip is proven deterministically in CI, not on hosted infrastructure | Claiming proven restore before that limitation closes would be a false trust claim — the most damaging kind for this brand |
| Demo never pretends | Explicit runtime mode; demo refuses archive actions | Demo state is labelled and archive actions are inert | — | Blurring the boundary breaks B6 |
| Nothing is invented | No inference, no classifier, no advice in the product | Every row was typed by the user | Cash and unrecorded spending are invisible | Adding inference without changing the brand claim would make the brand dishonest |

### Two live caveats the brand must respect today

1. **Hosted restore is unproven** (above). No brand expression may state or imply that
   restoring a real account has been demonstrated.
2. **Four mobile defects were fixed but not yet re-verified on a physical device**
   (a transient wrong aggregate, sheet/viewport behaviour, the amount-entry contract
   and the undo toast). Until the owner's bounded retest passes, the brand may claim
   the *properties* — which are enforced in code and tests — but no phase may cite
   physical-device proof for them.

**The rule:** the brand may claim a property the product enforces. It may not claim a
verification that has not happened.

## 16. Phase E strategic creative brief

**Phase E's task:** produce **three genuinely distinct creative territories** that all
satisfy the locked strategy. Phase E does not revisit strategy; it interprets it.

### The brief in one paragraph

MoneyFlow is the ledger a person keeps themselves, in Vietnamese, where every figure
can be followed back to a record they wrote and every ordinary mistake can be undone.
It competes against apps that remove the typing and ask for trust in return; it offers
the opposite trade. Express a product that is exact without being cold, calm without
being sleepy, and deferential without being empty.

### Must be true of all three territories

- Reads as a **personal ledger**, not a dashboard, bank, wallet, coach or assistant.
- Expresses **provenance and traceability** — the idea that a number has a source.
- Expresses **recoverable control** — mistakes are expected and survivable.
- Supports **Vietnamese as the native language**, including diacritics at real UI sizes
  and Vietnamese-length strings.
- Compatible with the personality's five NOTs — nothing urgent, judgemental,
  celebratory about balances, or all-knowing.
- Honours financial semantics that already exist in product law: money must never rely
  on colour alone; income, expense and transfer must remain distinguishable
  non-chromatically.
- Works for the **primary daily loop first** — record, position, history, period —
  with planning and advanced intake visibly secondary.

### Must not be assumed by any territory

- Bank connections, AI, automatic categorisation, investments, household sharing,
  envelope budgeting, gamification, or a safe-to-spend figure.
- Any specific palette, typeface, metaphor or layout inherited from Calm Ledger v2,
  Signal Ledger v3, Fresh Blue, the current shipped design system or any prior
  screenshot. **All of these are historical input.** Signal Ledger v3 is explicitly
  rejected as a direction and Calm Ledger v2 is explicitly historical; neither may
  return as authority through Phase E.
- Demographic characteristics of the audience.

### What makes the three territories genuinely distinct

They must differ in **interpretive stance**, not decoration. Three territories that
share one metaphor with different accent colours are one territory. Distinctness will
be judged on: what the visual language treats as the hero (the record? the account? the
period? the act of writing?), how it expresses traceability, and how it handles the
emotional register between *exact* and *unhurried*.

### Deliverable expectation

Three territories, each stating: its interpretive stance, how it satisfies each locked
element in §14, where it is deliberately risky, and what it would cost to build. No
selection — the owner selects.
