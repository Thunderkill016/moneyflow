# MoneyFlow — Neon Blue application directions

**Status:** owner selection required  
**Hue family held constant:** Neon Blue centered on `#3445FB`  
**Comparison rule:** same product truth, content, logo geometry and representative screens; only the application system changes

| Criterion | Weight |

## Direction A — Precision Core

### Model

A white-first product where Neon Blue is a controlled operational signal. The brand is vivid, but it appears mainly where the user can act, focus or confirm location.

### Information and interaction

- Landing: neutral hero with Neon Blue logo arms, one filled CTA, active links and small numbered story signals.
- Authentication: neutral form, one Neon Blue submit action, visible focus, restrained proof rail.
- Workspace: neutral shell, Neon Blue active navigation marker and primary transaction action.
- Accounts/transactions: selection uses a pale Neon tint plus border, never a fully saturated card.
- Charts: Neon Blue marks the selected or primary series; financial semantic series retain independent colors.

### Visual language

- 80–88% neutral surfaces.
- 8–12% Neon Blue.
- Crisp borders, low elevation and compact high-contrast controls.
- Primary logo: Neon Blue flow arms, dark gate and wordmark.
- Inverse logo used only on a deliberate Neon Blue brand surface.

### Representative screen behavior

| Surface | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Landing hero | White canvas, dark headline, one Neon CTA, small Neon eyebrow/step marks |
| Guided story | Alternating neutral sections with Neon step number and link |
| Login/recovery | White form card, Neon submit/focus, dark text |
| Workspace shell | Neutral sidebar/topbar, thin Neon active marker |
| Account summary | Neutral cards; semantic values stay green/red/violet |
| Transaction selection | Pale Neon surface + Neon border + icon/label |
| Chart | Neon primary series with direct labels; other series use separate ordered palette |

### Strengths

- Highest financial-content readability.
- Strongest mobile discipline.
- Lowest risk of promotional-looking workspace screens.
- Easiest to maintain through semantic tokens.
- Clear separation between brand and financial states.

### Risks

- May feel too conservative if typography, composition and product proof remain weak.
- The owner may perceive Neon Blue as insufficiently visible.
- Requires excellent neutral hierarchy; color cannot rescue a flat layout.

### Reject this direction when

The desired identity requires large unmistakable brand surfaces in most first impressions.

## Direction B — Neon Frame

### Model

Neon Blue becomes a visible framing material. It establishes strong branded regions around otherwise neutral product content.

### Information and interaction

- Landing: a Neon Blue hero frame or top stage holds the inverse logo, headline support and primary CTA; product proof remains on white.
- Authentication: a Neon Blue proof panel frames a neutral form panel.
- Workspace: a compact Neon Blue navigation rail or header frame identifies the product; the content canvas stays neutral.
- Accounts/transactions: active page and major action are framed by Neon regions, while financial rows remain neutral.
- Charts: selected report context may use a Neon header/frame, but plotted financial series remain independent.

### Visual language

- 65–75% neutral surfaces.
- 15–22% Neon Blue, concentrated in a few structural regions.
- Strong rectangular framing, inverse logo and high-contrast white-on-Neon typography.
- Reduced reliance on shadows; separation comes from color blocks and borders.

### Representative screen behavior

| Surface | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Landing hero | Neon Blue stage with inverse mark; white product-proof panel overlaps or follows |
| Guided story | Neutral content with a recurring Neon side rail or chapter band |
| Login/recovery | Split layout: Neon proof panel + neutral form |
| Workspace shell | Neon navigation frame with neutral content canvas |
| Account summary | Neutral cards inside a branded shell; semantic amounts unchanged |
| Transaction selection | Neutral rows; selected row uses pale tint, not full Neon fill |
| Chart | Neutral chart area inside Neon report frame/header |

### Strengths

- Most immediately recognizable as a rebrand.
- Gives the B3.2 inverse logo a clear role.
- Creates a strong public-to-workspace visual bridge.
- Makes Neon Blue feel like the main brand color without recoloring financial content.

### Risks

- Large high-chroma regions can dominate small screens.
- Dark workspace and Neon frame transitions need careful tuning.
- More layout-specific than Direction A; harder to apply consistently to every route.
- White text and controls on Neon must be measured on every effective surface.

### Reject this direction when

The workspace must remain extremely quiet for long daily sessions or when the physical-device review shows fatigue on saturated regions.

## Direction C — Editorial Current

### Model

Neon Blue behaves like an editorial current through typography, chapter markers, dividers and data emphasis rather than large background fills. The product feels more expressive without turning the workspace into a marketing page.

### Information and interaction

- Landing: large dark typography, Neon Blue words/underlines/chapter numerals and one decisive CTA.
- Authentication: Neon editorial title and progress/recovery cues; form remains neutral.
- Workspace: Neon page titles, route marker, key action and selected data label; navigation frame remains neutral.
- Accounts/transactions: strong typographic hierarchy, tabular numerals and direct labels; selection uses line, icon and subtle surface.
- Charts: Neon is used for the current comparison or annotation, not automatically for the largest series.

### Visual language

- 75–82% neutral surfaces.
- 10–16% Neon Blue across type, rules, numerals and actions.
- Larger type scale, visible section rhythm and fewer card containers.
- Brand identity comes from composition and editorial pacing, not only filled buttons.

### Representative screen behavior

| Surface | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Landing hero | Dark headline with selected Neon phrase, chapter line and Neon CTA |
| Guided story | Large Neon chapter numbers and direct product evidence |
| Login/recovery | Neutral form with Neon title/accent rule and strong recovery hierarchy |
| Workspace shell | Neutral navigation; Neon route title/marker and primary action |
| Account summary | Typographic groups with fewer cards and clearly labeled semantic values |
| Transaction selection | Neon leading rule + icon + subtle tint; keyboard focus remains separate |
| Chart | Neutral series palette with Neon annotation/current comparison |

### Strengths

- Most distinctive without requiring large saturated surfaces.
- Can improve hierarchy and reduce card-heavy UI.
- Works well with long Vietnamese copy when line lengths are controlled.
- Preserves financial semantic separation.

### Risks

- Requires broader typography and layout work; not a simple palette migration.
- Poor execution can feel inconsistent across dense workspace screens.
- More subjective and harder to lock through source tests alone.
- Large type must be validated carefully at `320px` and 200% text.

### Reject this direction when

The scope is strictly limited to token migration and cannot support a coordinated typography/composition update.

## Comparison summary

| Direction | Core idea | Neon usage | Best quality | Main risk |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| A — Precision Core | controlled operational signal | 8–12% | clarity and daily usability | may feel too restrained |
| B — Neon Frame | visible structural brand surfaces | 15–22% | strongest immediate identity | saturation and mobile dominance |
| C — Editorial Current | typographic and compositional current | 10–16% | distinctive hierarchy | broader redesign scope |

## Recommendation before owner selection

**Direction A is the safest system baseline. Direction C is the strongest candidate for a more distinctive MoneyFlow identity. Direction B should be selected only when the owner explicitly wants large visible Neon Blue surfaces and accepts the higher mobile/dark-mode validation burden.**

The next gate is owner selection of A, B or C—or a request for another divergence round. No token migration or production implementation is authorized by this document.
